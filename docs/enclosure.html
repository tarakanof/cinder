Knob Enclosure

A 3D-printed desk puck for the VIEWE UEDX46460015-MD50ET knob (ESP32-S3, 1.5" AMOLED) and its USB-test-MD50-V5.1 adapter. Four variants share one shell. Status: modelled and meshed, not printed yet. Numbers come from the vendor drawing (spec V2.0 p.9). Values tagged EST were scaled from the drawing or a photo; measure them before the final print.

50–150 mAaverage at 5 V (spec p.8); Wi-Fi TX peaks ≈300+ mA
Hold the hub, not the body. The drawing puts 3× M2.5 screw holes and 2× Ø2.0 "pillars used for positioning" on the rear hub, and the FPC leaves through the hub centre, so the hub is the fixed mount. What turns is less certain: the vendor's exploded view (ABS base → encoder/press block → PCB → display → CNC metal casing) shows the FPC running through the base to the PCB, so most likely only the CNC ring turns and the display stays put. The design does not depend on the answer: the puck seats the hub on a ledge, clamps it with a washer, and touches nothing else. The body floats 1.0 mm above the deck with 0.6 mm radial clearance. A stand that grips the body (as most M5Dial/CrowPanel stands do) could lock the ring. Check on your unit: hold the hub, turn the ring, and press to hear the click.

Recommendation

Print variant (a) first: shell + washer + lid_a. It keeps USB data for flashing and logs, costs only filament and screws, and tests every estimated dimension. Before the full print, cut the shell in PrusaSlicer at z = 17 and print only the top ring (about 30 min, estimate) to check the hub fit and the body clearance.

Then, if you want the knob to lift off the desk, add variant (b): a magnetic pogo dock. It still carries USB data. Skip (c) Qi: it gives no data, wastes about a third of the energy as heat, and can cause ghost touches. Build (d) battery only if the knob must leave the desk; it is 13.5 mm taller and runs about 6 h at typical brightness.

Peaks. The spec currents are averages with the backlight on. ESP32-S3 Wi-Fi TX bursts reach about 300+ mA (estimated at the 5 V input). The PowerBoost 1000C (1 A) and USB have ample headroom. The Adafruit 1901 Qi receiver (500 mA) has about 1.5× headroom at the peak: add 220–470 µF across 5 V/GND at J2 so short bursts do not brown it out. In (b), the magnetic receptacle must fit within lid_t + standoff = 6.5 mm under the board EST.

Runtime = mAh × 3.7 V × 0.85 boost efficiency ÷ (5 V × load). Spec p.8: 4.0–5.5 V supply, 50 / 100 / 150 mA (min / typ / max) at full backlight, 50 mA typ with the backlight off; recommended supply 5 V 1 A.

Battery notes

  • Size limit. A rectangle fits a round bay only if its diagonal is smaller than the bay. 803040 (50 mm diagonal) fits the Ø78 cup. A 503450 (60.5 mm) does not fit inside the Ø66 shell.
  • Avoid IP5306 boards without I2C. They switch the output off when the load stays under 45 mA for 32 s, and this knob idles near 50 mA. TP4056 + MT3608 works but has no load-sharing.
  • Sleep stretches it. CO5300 has sleep-in (0x10) and display-off (0x28). On AMOLED, black pixels draw almost nothing. ESP32-S3 auto light-sleep with Wi-Fi at DTIM3 is about 1.3 mA (Espressif datasheet). With the panel asleep, expect 3–8 mA from the cell EST, so about 5–14 days of standby.
  • Battery sensing. The FPC carries GPIO39/40 (J2 pins 4/5, labels still disputed), 43/44 (UART) and 19/20 (USB). The ESP32-S3 datasheet (table 2-8) gives ADC only on GPIO1–20, so 39/40/43/44 cannot read a voltage, and 19/20 are the USB pair. Use a MAX17048 on I2C over GPIO39/40 after a continuity test of J2 pins 4/5, or the PowerBoost LBO pin as a digital low-battery flag (3.2 V).
  • Diode. Feed (c) or (d) into J2 pin 1 through a Schottky diode, so that a USB-C cable in the adapter cannot back-feed the Qi module or the boost converter.

What you must measure

Use calipers. Enter the values in the [params] block of enclosure/knob_puck.scad and run python3 enclosure/make_stl.py.

  • Body OD at 3 angles; enter the largest as body_d. The pocket is body_d + 1.2.
  • Hub steps: OD and length of each step, most of all hub2_h EST. This sets the 1.0 mm gap under the body. Also measure the latch-tab OD hub_tab_d EST.
  • Pillars: protrusion pin_h and circle pin_pcd. Screw holes: circle scr_pcd and depth (screw length).
  • Rotation and press: hold the hub and turn the body. Measure the press travel (it must be under 1.0 mm).
  • Adapter: ad_w, ad_d, the 4 hole centres ad_holes and their size, the USB-C centre from the J1 edge usb_y, and whether the receptacle is top-mount (usb_zc 1.6) or mid-mount (about 0).
  • FPC: real length from the hub face, and which face carries the contacts. Rotate the knob in the socket to get the correct face at J1 after the U-turn.
  • Your USB-C plug: overmold width × height must be under 13 × 11.4.
  • (b) pogo connector body pogo_l × pogo_w and height. (c) Qi panel size. (d) cell and board size.

Print settings (Prusa, 0.4 nozzle)

PartOrientationSettings
shellpocket up0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters, 5 top / 4 bottom, 20 % gyroid. The cavity roof is a 43 mm bridge; sag is not verified. A 0.2 mm membrane closes the Ø18 ledge hole, so no hole edge prints over air: cut it out with a knife afterwards. If the roof sags, re-slice with "supports on build plate only".
washergroove up0.15 mm, 100 % infill. Under the pillar groove only 1.3 mm of washer is left under the screw heads, so use M2.5 flat washers. The screw holes are radial slots (r 6.05–7.52), so they fit either reading of the screw circle (Ø12.10 or Ø15.03).
lid_a/b/c/dstandoffs up0.2 mm, 4 perimeters (the standoffs take the M2 screws). Recesses face the bed; elephant-foot compensation 0.2. lid_c's Qi recess roof is a 32 mm bridge (span across 48 mm); a little sag only pushes the panel down, toward the pad.
dock_bseat up0.2 mm, 15 % infill. Optional: pause at the layer above the magnet pockets to cover the magnets.
riser_dfloor down0.2 mm, 3 perimeters.

Material. PLA for (a), (b) and (d). PETG for (c), because the Qi coil gets warm and PLA softens near 60 °C. Supports: none. Fits (FDM, per side): 0.3 general, 0.6 radial at the body, magnets +0.15 for press fit (+0.2 for glue), M3 self-tapping into Ø2.5 pilots, M2 into Ø1.7.

Assembly, variant (a)

  1. Drop the knob into the shell. The hub shoulder must sit on the ledge. Turn the body: it must not touch anything. Press it: you must hear the click.
  2. Cut the 0.2 mm membrane out of the ledge hole. From below, slide the washer (groove up) over the FPC. Turn the knob until its 3 holes line up with the washer slots. Fit 3× M2.5×6 with flat washers.
  3. Screw the adapter to the lid standoffs, components up, with 4× M2×4 (×5 is too long: the tip would reach the pilot bottom). The USB-C goes on the lid side without a screw hole.
  4. Hold the lid next to the shell and connect the FPC to J1. Lift the lid into the shell, folding the slack into the service loop toward +y. Nothing may press the FPC against the screw heads.
  5. Fit 3× M3×8 self-tapping through the lid. Stick 4 bumpers into the recesses. Plug USB-C in through the port.

(b) Push the pogo receptacle into lid_b and solder it to J2 pin 1 (5V), 3 (GND), 9 (DP) and 10 (DN). These four pins agree in every vendor source. Before you press the magnets in, hold each dock magnet against its puck partner and confirm that they attract. (c) Glue the Qi panel coil-down into the lid recess, then run 5V through a diode to J2 pin 1 and GND to pin 3. (d) Use M3×22 through the cup, lid and shell.
